# Evidence Source Protocol

## Contents

1. Match evidence effort to consequence
2. Prefer fit and authority
3. Record transferable claims
4. Search and verify responsibly
5. Report bounded recommendations

## 1. Match evidence effort to consequence

Use instructor-approved rationale for routine, reversible choices. For consequential pedagogy, assessment, accessibility, safety, or policy decisions, identify authoritative or research support and its limits. Increase source quality, triangulation, and review as stakes rise.

## 2. Prefer fit and authority

Use this order while preserving the instructor's educational authority:

1. applicable course and institutional requirements;
2. applicable disciplinary, professional, safety, accreditation, or technical standards;
3. recent systematic reviews, consensus reports, or strong syntheses;
4. relevant primary studies in comparable populations, disciplines, and modalities;
5. credible practice guidance with disclosed context;
6. illustrative or generated ideas labeled as provisional.

Do not treat source rank as sufficient. Examine population, prior knowledge, discipline, modality, task, outcome measure, implementation conditions, and plausible harms.

## 3. Record transferable claims

Use `assets/source-register.md`. For each consequential recommendation, record:

- the exact claim supported;
- source authority and study type;
- population, discipline, modality, and conditions;
- strength: strong, promising, context-dependent, contested, or unknown;
- limitations and transfer risks;
- implementation cost and plausible downside;
- evidence after use that would justify keeping, revising, or stopping the design.

Example supported record:

```markdown
Claim: Retrieval followed by corrective feedback can strengthen later access to prerequisite concepts.
Fit: Introductory course; low-stakes practice; later opportunity to use the concept.
Boundary: Does not show that recall alone meets the transfer outcome.
Decision: Use a brief retrieval prompt, feedback, and a new application.
```

Example context-dependent record:

```markdown
Claim: Peer discussion may improve explanation quality.
Fit risk: Large fixed-seat course with limited facilitation and uneven preparation.
Decision: Pilot structured pairs with individual commitment and an exit check; do not assume discussion quality from participation.
```

## 4. Search and verify responsibly

Formulate the educational question before searching. Prefer primary or authoritative sources for current policy, technical standards, and factual claims. For learning research, begin with strong syntheses and inspect the underlying population and outcome. Record stable references, retrieval dates for changing material, and reuse restrictions.

Do not cite a source solely from a search snippet or secondary paraphrase when the original is available. Do not use citation count, venue prestige, or confident wording as a substitute for fit and quality.

## 5. Report bounded recommendations

Describe the recommendation, mechanism, evidence strength, applicability, cost, downside, and evidence-to-collect. Use `evidence-informed` only when the supporting rationale or source is recorded. Otherwise say `provisional design rationale` or `instructor-approved practice`.
