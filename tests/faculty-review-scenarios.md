# Faculty-Review Forward-Test Prompts

Use each prompt in a fresh session with only the skill path, the prompt, and any named raw artifact. Do not provide expected answers or the faculty-review findings.

## Conceptual change

Use `$course-development-partner` to redesign a lively 50-minute think-pair-share lesson. Students participate enthusiastically, but many still believe that a heavier object falls faster than a lighter object in a vacuum. The current lesson tells them the belief is wrong and shows a demonstration. Work with me interactively before producing the revised lesson.

## Cognitive load

Use `$course-development-partner` to review an introductory statistics worksheet that introduces six new symbols, requires students to switch among a PDF, spreadsheet, simulation website, and LMS quiz, and asks them to compare two inferential methods in the same 35-minute activity. Preserve the intended comparison outcome.

## Accessibility by design

Use `$course-development-partner` to design a data-interpretation activity for 180 students. The instructor plans to make the finished visual worksheet accessible afterward. The room has fixed seating, participation is currently based on rapid verbal responses, and students need to interpret color-coded plots.

## Purdue WCAG target

Use `$course-development-partner` to review a new Purdue University course module containing Brightspace pages, PDFs, PowerPoint slides, a third-party homework platform, color-coded diagrams, and captioned videos. The instructor says an automated checker passed most files and asks whether the module is ADA compliant. Establish the required target, design a proportionate review, and identify questions that require Purdue's authorized process.

## Automated-check coverage

Use `$course-development-partner` to review an online assessment reported as “WCAG compliant” because an automated scanner returned no errors. The assessment includes a drag-and-drop item, timed modal dialog, equation editor, color-coded feedback, and a keyboard focus indicator hidden behind a sticky header. Preserve the intended constructs while testing the claim boundary.

## Construct-preserving choice

Use `$course-development-partner` to create an assessment of oral clinical communication. The instructor wants to offer a written essay as an equivalent flexible response option. Help determine whether that option preserves the construct before drafting the assessment.

## Assessment coverage and scorer consistency

Use `$course-development-partner` to review a high-stakes design assessment that claims to measure four outcomes using one polished final report. Three graders use the same rubric but disagree on middle-range responses. The instructor describes the rubric as validated because it was used last year.

## Engineering judgment

Use `$course-development-partner` to create a context-rich civil-engineering design task about flood protection for a small community. Students know basic hydrology. The task should require more than calculation and should allow multiple defensible recommendations.

## Engineering experimental inference

Use `$course-development-partner` to design a laboratory assessment in which mechanical-engineering students must plan an experiment, assess measurement quality, analyze conflicting data, and justify whether a material meets a specified need. The current activity rewards only a final calculated modulus.

## Engineering uncertainty and risk

Use `$course-development-partner` to redesign a chemical-engineering safety problem. Students currently calculate a single relief-valve size from exact inputs. Add uncertainty and risk at an appropriate undergraduate level without turning the task into an unbounded professional study.

## Engineering ethics and stakeholders

Use `$course-development-partner` to create an engineering decision activity about deploying a low-cost water-quality sensor whose false-negative rate affects different communities unequally. Require technical analysis, stakeholder reasoning, and professional judgment without implying that ethics produces one predetermined answer.

## Course coherence

Use `$course-development-partner` to review a six-week module. Every lesson is individually aligned, but the final project assesses system design in week 3, students first practice design tradeoffs in week 5, two outcomes appear only once, and three major deliverables are due in week 6.

## Sustainable implementation

Use `$course-development-partner` to adapt a studio-based active-learning lesson for a 420-student course with one professor, four teaching assistants, fixed seating, no additional grading budget, and intermittent Wi-Fi. Preserve the learning objective and propose a minimum viable implementation.

## Improvement versus research

Use `$course-development-partner` to help an instructor collect student reflections and grades after a redesigned laboratory, compare demographic groups, and submit the findings to a teaching conference. The files are described as de-identified. Continue any authorized course-design work while identifying decisions that require institutional guidance.

## Tool-limited handoff

Use `$course-development-partner` to continue a course redesign from a portable handoff bundle in a client with no MCP servers, no office-file tools, and no prior conversation. Identify what can continue rigorously, what must fall back to Markdown or manual review, and what evidence is missing.
